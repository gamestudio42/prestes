# Chapter 7 - The Pantanal

> **Label:** dramatized. The column's crossing of the Pantanal in
> January-February 1927 and the Paraguay/Bolivia split are historical; the
> prose, the marsh and the crossing are a dramatization. The main body entered
> Bolivia at San Matias on 3 February 1927; Siqueira’s group entered Paraguay in
> March. **Tone reference:** Jorge Amado, _O Cavaleiro da Esperança_. The prose
> is original; each page quotation is attributed with its edition and page,
> shown under the page in the English and Portuguese text below
> (complete-books-and-bibliography).

## English

### The Road to the Border

At the end of October 1926 the high command decided how the march would end.
Djalma Dutra and Moreira Lima went south to find Isidoro. The column would wait
on the Brazilian side until the end of January and, with no word from them,
cross into Bolivia at the beginning of February (Macaulay, p. 232).

On 24 December, near Presidente Murtinho, about four hundred Mato Grosso
militiamen, most of them green recruits, blocked the road. João Alberto and Ary
Salgado Freire turned their flank and scattered them, at the cost of one man. It
was the last loyalist force between the column and the border; the jagunços of
Franklin de Albuquerque kept pressing on the rear (p. 233).

### The Marsh

The column crossed the Paraguay near its headwaters and turned south. On 10
January 1927 it entered the Pantanal (Blanc, p. 127). First came dry forest, cut
through by hand; then the floodplain. The men lost almost all their horses. They
marched at times in water up to their chests, slept on anthills, the only dry
ground, under clouds of mosquitoes, and ate hearts of palm when the cattle gave
out (Macaulay, p. 234).

> We no longer had a single dry piece of clothing. It was impossible! We dreamed of finding a bit of solid ground, where we could make a fire…
>
> — João Alberto Lins de Barros, memoir, on the Pantanal, January 1927
>
> `blanc-2024`, The Prestes Column (Duke, 2024), p. 127. Blanc's translation from the Portuguese.

### The Border

On 3 February 1927 the column crossed the unmarked frontier and camped north of
San Matías. Ary Salgado Freire rode into the Bolivian town to arrange the
surrender and found Moreira Lima there, back from Argentina. On 4 February 620
men handed over ninety Mausers, four machine guns, two automatic rifles and
eight thousand cartridges (Macaulay, p. 234).

Siqueira Campos was not with them. He had lost touch with the column at the end
of October, learned at a telegraph office in Goiás on 8 February that it was in
Bolivia, and crossed into Paraguay with sixty-five men on 23 March. The march
was over.

> We were reduced to about six hundred bedraggled men, without shoes and half-naked. . . . The passage [in the Pantanal] was the most difficult of our whole campaign.
>
> — Italo Landucci, memoir (1947)
>
> `blanc-2024`, The Prestes Column (Duke, 2024), p. 127. Blanc's translation from the Portuguese.

## Português (BR)

### O Caminho da Fronteira

No fim de outubro de 1926 o alto comando decidiu como a marcha terminaria.
Djalma Dutra e Moreira Lima foram para o sul procurar Isidoro. A coluna
esperaria do lado brasileiro até o fim de janeiro e, sem notícias deles,
entraria na Bolívia no começo de fevereiro (Macaulay, p. 232).

Em 24 de dezembro, perto de Presidente Murtinho, uns quatrocentos milicianos de
Mato Grosso, quase todos recrutas, barraram a estrada. João Alberto e Ary
Salgado Freire os atacaram pelo flanco e os dispersaram, ao custo de um homem.
Era a última força legalista entre a coluna e a fronteira; os jagunços de
Franklin de Albuquerque continuavam pressionando a retaguarda (p. 233).

### O Alagado

A coluna atravessou o Paraguai perto das cabeceiras e virou para o sul. Em 10 de
janeiro de 1927 entrou no Pantanal (Blanc, p. 127). Primeiro veio a mata seca,
aberta a facão; depois a planície alagada. Os homens perderam quase todos os
cavalos. Marcharam às vezes com água pelo peito, dormiram sobre cupinzeiros, o
único chão seco, sob nuvens de mosquitos, e comeram palmito quando o gado acabou
(Macaulay, p. 234).

> Já não tínhamos uma única peça de roupa seca. Era impossível! Sonhávamos encontrar um pedaço de chão firme, onde pudéssemos fazer fogo…
>
> — João Alberto Lins de Barros, memórias, sobre o Pantanal, janeiro de 1927
>
> `blanc-2024`, The Prestes Column (Duke, 2024), p. 127. Tradução nossa da versão inglesa de Blanc; o original em português não foi consultado.

### A Fronteira

Em 3 de fevereiro de 1927 a coluna cruzou a fronteira sem marcos e acampou ao
norte de San Matías. Ary Salgado Freire foi à cidade boliviana acertar a
rendição e encontrou lá Moreira Lima, de volta da Argentina. Em 4 de fevereiro,
620 homens entregaram noventa Mausers, quatro metralhadoras, dois
fuzis-metralhadores e oito mil cartuchos (Macaulay, p. 234).

Siqueira Campos não estava com eles. Tinha perdido o contato com a coluna no fim
de outubro, soube num telégrafo de Goiás, em 8 de fevereiro, que ela estava na
Bolívia, e entrou no Paraguai com sessenta e cinco homens em 23 de março. A
marcha tinha acabado.

> Estávamos reduzidos a uns seiscentos homens maltrapilhos, descalços e seminus. . . . A travessia [do Pantanal] foi a mais difícil de toda a nossa campanha.
>
> — Italo Landucci, memórias (1947)
>
> `blanc-2024`, The Prestes Column (Duke, 2024), p. 127. Tradução nossa da versão inglesa de Blanc; o original em português não foi consultado.

## Sources and labels

- **Historical:** the column's crossing of the Pantanal in January-February 1927;
  the main body entering Bolivia at San Matias on 3 February; Siqueira’s group
  entering Paraguay in March; the march dissolving into exile.
- **Dramatized:** the marsh route, the crossings, the fatigue and the dialogue.
- **Fictional:** the individual soldiers and the exact route.
- **Sources:** `macaulay-1974`, ch. IX, pp. 230-234; `blanc-2024` (social and
  interior history); `anita-1991` (citation-only).
