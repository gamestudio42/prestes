# Chapter 7 - The Pantanal

> **Label:** dramatized. The column's crossing of the Pantanal in January-February
> 1927 and the Paraguay/Bolivia split are historical; the prose, the marsh and
> the crossing are a dramatization. The main body entered Bolivia at San Matias
> on 3 February 1927; Siqueira’s group entered Paraguay in March. **Tone
> reference:** Jorge Amado, _O Cavaleiro da Esperança_. No text is reproduced;
> the prose is original.

## English

### The Marsh

February 1927. The column entered the Pantanal. The water was everywhere; the
ground was a suggestion. The men were exhausted, the animals were failing, and
the border was the only objective left. There was no ground worth holding and no
enemy worth a battle. The march itself was the enemy.

### The Crossing

The column moved through marsh, water and cattle trails. The wounded and the
supply animals fell behind; the rearguard decided what to carry and what to
leave. The government patrols were a threat, but the heat and the water were
closer. The column kept moving because stopping was the end.

### The Border

The main body reached the border and entered Bolivia; Siqueira’s group entered
Paraguay. The march dissolved into exile. The men who had crossed the south, the
Paraná and the interior were now exiles. The column was still together, but the
march was over.

## Português (BR)

### O Pantanal

Fevereiro de 1927. A coluna entrou no Pantanal. A água estava em todo lugar; o
chão era uma sugestão. Os homens estavam exaustos, os animais falhando, e a
fronteira era o único objetivo que restava. Não havia terreno que valesse a pena
segurar nem inimigo que valesse uma batalha. A própria marcha era o inimigo.

### A Travessia

A coluna seguiu por pantanal, água e trilhas de gado. Os feridos e os animais de
carga ficaram para trás; a retaguarda decidiu o que levar e o que deixar. As
patrulhas do governo eram uma ameaça, mas o calor e a água estavam mais perto. A
coluna continuou andando porque parar era o fim.

### A Fronteira

O corpo principal chegou à fronteira e entrou na Bolívia; o grupo de Siqueira
entrou no Paraguai. A marcha se dissolveu no exílio. Os homens que haviam cruzado
o sul, o Paraná e o interior agora eram exilados. A coluna ainda estava junta, mas
a marcha tinha acabado.
