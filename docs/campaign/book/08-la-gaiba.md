# Chapter 8 - La Gaiba

> **Label:** dramatized. The exiles' monument at La Gaiba, Bolivia, is historical:
> the granite reads “Glory to the Bandeirantes of Freedom, 1924–1927.” The 5 July
> 1927 date is a secondary tradition; the prose, the ceremony and the choices are
> a dramatization. **Tone reference:** Jorge Amado, _O Cavaleiro da Esperança_.
> The prose is original apart from one attributed quotation of Moreira Lima
> (public domain).

## English

### The Exile

La Gaiba, Bolivia, July 1927. The march was over. The column had crossed the
Pantanal and entered exile; the men who had walked from the south were now
scattered across a foreign border. There was no enemy left to fight and no ground
left to hold. There was only the memory of the march.

### The Monument

The exiles dedicated a monument. It was not a victory monument; it was a stone
for the march, for the dead, for the towns and the rivers and the men who had not
come. On the granite they cut: “Glory to the Bandeirantes of Freedom, 1924–1927.”
The ceremony was small, and the words mattered because there was nothing else
left to build.

> Soldiers of Liberty! Sleep in peace in the foreign land that took you in so nobly, for your names and your deeds will live for ever in the generous heart of Brazil.
>
> — Lourenço Moreira Lima, La Gaiba, Bolivia, 1927

(Translated from the Portuguese. Cite: Marchas e combates, 3rd ed. (1979), p. 523, via Mozzer 2009, p. 257.)

### The Memory

The campaign ends here. The column is memory. The record must say what the march
cost and what it was: a political march that survived the government and did not
become a victory. The monument can hold only so much. The rest is the record.

## Português (BR)

### O Exílio

La Gaiba, Bolívia, julho de 1927. A marcha tinha acabado. A coluna cruzara o
Pantanal e entrara no exílio; os homens que caminharam desde o sul estavam agora
espalhados por uma fronteira estrangeira. Não havia inimigo a enfrentar nem chão a
segurar. Havia apenas a memória da marcha.

### O Monumento

Os exilados dedicaram um monumento. Não era um monumento de vitória; era uma
pedra para a marcha, para os mortos, para as cidades e os rios e os homens que
não vieram. No granito, gravaram: “Glória aos Bandeirantes da Liberdade,
1924–1927.” A cerimônia foi pequena, e as palavras importavam porque não havia
mais nada para construir.

> Soldados da Liberdade! Dormi tranqüilos na terra estrangeira que vos acolheu com tanta nobreza, porque os vossos nomes e os vossos feitos serão eternos no coração generoso do Brasil.
>
> — Lourenço Moreira Lima, La Gaiba, Bolívia, 1927

(Cite: Marchas e combates, 3rd ed. (1979), p. 523, via Mozzer 2009, p. 257.)

### A Memória

A campanha termina aqui. A coluna é memória. O registro deve dizer quanto a marcha
custou e o que ela foi: uma marcha política que sobreviveu ao governo e não se
tornou vitória. O monumento só pode guardar tanta coisa. O resto é o registro.

## Sources and labels

- **Historical:** the column's exile in Bolivia in 1927; the exiles' monument at
  La Gaiba, inscribed “Glory to the Bandeirantes of Freedom, 1924–1927”; the end
  of the march.
- **Dramatized:** the ceremony, the speeches and the choices; the 5 July 1927
  date is a secondary tradition.
- **Fictional:** the individual exiles and the exact words.
- **Sources:** `blanc-2024`, ch. 7 and fig. 7.5 (monument and inscription);
  `cpdoc-coluna` (La Gaiba–Libres split); `macaulay-1974`, ch. IX, pp. 230-234
  (road to exile; no La Gaiba); `anita-1991` (citation-only).
- **Rights:** no Amado text is reproduced. The historical photographs supplied
  for the compendium are period newspaper or document images over 100 years old;
  the project owner determined them public domain on 2026-11-06.
