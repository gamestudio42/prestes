### Prologue - Os Dezoito do Forte

- [x] Forte command: the commander was taken prisoner while negotiating; First
      Lieutenant Siqueira Campos took command. Macaulay, ch. I, pp. 26–29. The
      fort’s pre-sortie garrison was 29.
- [ ] What was the fort's exact layout and armament, and did government forces
      use artillery or naval fire? Macaulay mentions the fort’s long-range coast
      artillery firing before the sortie; the detailed plan is still open.
- [x] Beach route and final positions: Macaulay, ch. I, pp. 26–29 — the avenue,
      the Londres Hotel, the Atlântica/Barroso line, the sidewalk and the
      construction pipes. House numbers and minute-by-minute timing remain open.
- [x] Survivors: Siqueira Campos, one other officer and one enlisted man
      survived; seven rebels were killed or died of wounds. Macaulay, ch. I,
      pp. 26–29. The legend says all eighteen died; the record does not.
- [ ] The four groups of five, five, four and four are a design compression of
      the legend; Macaulay has 28 marching and 16 at contact. Keep the design
      choice, but do not present the groups as the historical order of battle.
- [ ] Did the fort have a working telegraph or signal link to Realengo or the
      city? The plan included cutting lines; the fort's communications are
      unclear.
- [x] Ammunition: Macaulay says each rebel received a shoulder arm and at least
      two hundred rounds. The 5 + 45 load is a playability abstraction.

## Forces (`docs/history/FORCES.md`)

- [x] Did the police fight for the government? Yes: the state militias (each
      state's police and army at once) outnumbered the federal army in 1925,
      and several fought the column; in São Paulo in July 1924 the state
      militia split between the two sides. Macaulay, pp. 9–10, 19, 24, 71, 90.
- [ ] What were the state forces called in Portuguese at the time, beyond the
      Brigada Militar of Rio Grande do Sul (Macaulay, p. 48)? The São Paulo
      force above all. Macaulay gives them in English as "state militia".
- [ ] What were the gaúcho provisionals and the federal light infantry called
      in Portuguese ("provisórios", "corpos provisórios", "caçadores")? Needs
      a Portuguese-language source.
- [ ] What did the federal army and each state militia wear in 1924–1927?
      Macaulay describes only the gaúcho rebels' dress (pp. 44, 52) and says
      one Patriotic Battalion was issued federal uniforms (p. 189).
- [ ] How was a platoon or section of federal infantry or of a state militia
      made up? Macaulay gives units and totals, not small-unit tables; the
      game's section types for them are a game organization until a source
      says otherwise.
- [ ] How strong was a typical Patriotic Battalion, and how was it armed
      beyond the one recorded shipment (Macaulay, p. 205)?
