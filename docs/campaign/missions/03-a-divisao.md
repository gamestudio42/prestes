## Pitch

The southern column and the São Paulo remnants meet in western Paraná in April 1925. They are two defeated forces, not yet one army. Prestes and Miguel Costa
meet at Benjamin Constant on 3 April and agree to combine; the division is
formally organized at Porto Santa Helena on 14 April; Foz do Iguaçu is Isidoro’s
political headquarters, not the junction. Miguel Costa takes formal command;
Prestes becomes chief of staff. The player must turn the two brigades into the
First Revolutionary Division and get it across the Paraná, with its men, horses
and guns, before the government closes the border. The division is born; the
march continues.

Target length: 20-25 minutes at 1x, in one sitting.

## Historical frame

In April 1925 the southern and São Paulo forces combined in western Paraná. The
meeting of Prestes and Miguel Costa was at **Benjamin Constant do Paraná on 3
April**; they agreed to move to Porto Santa Helena, and then conferred with
Marshal Isidoro at Foz do Iguaçu. The First Revolutionary Division was formally
organized on **14 April 1925**. Macaulay gives about 1,500 men, 600 horses and
mules, and three 75-mm guns crossing from Porto Mendes to Puerto Adela: the
landing began on 27 April and the crossing was completed on 29 April. Prestes's
Rio Grande Brigade had roughly 800 men; the São Paulo Brigade roughly 700. Miguel
Costa was the division's formal commander; Prestes was chief of staff and the
column's most visible leader.

### Historical, dramatized, fictional

- **Historical:** the April 1925 combination in western Paraná; the Benjamin
  Constant meeting on 3 April; the Foz conference with Isidoro; the First
  Revolutionary Division formally organized on 14 April; Miguel Costa as formal
  commander; Prestes as chief of staff; the Rio Grande and São Paulo brigades;
  the crossing from Porto Mendes to Puerto Adela, landing 27 April and completed
  29 April; about 1,500 men, 600 horses and mules, and three 75-mm guns.
- **Dramatized:** the exact order of the junction, the crossing sequence, the
  government pursuit and the dialogue.
- **Fictional:** the individual soldiers and the exact order of the boats,
  horses and guns. The playable slice is a command section, not the whole
  division.
- **Command structure:** Miguel Costa is the formal commander; Prestes is chief
  of staff. The mission must not reduce the division to Prestes.

## Perspective

Miguel Costa, the formal commander of the First Revolutionary Division. The
player sees the junction from the command side: two columns with different
histories, a river, a government pursuit and a command structure that has to
work. Prestes is present as chief of staff and the division's most visible
leader, but the mission is Miguel Costa's. This is the campaign's corrective to
the idea that the column was one man.

## Player fantasy and pillar

- **Fantasy:** Turn two defeated columns into one division and get it across the
  Paraná before the government closes the border.
- **Pillar:** 1, command the section, not the hero; 3, the march costs more than
  the battle; 4, history with receipts.
- **Loop beat:** Command. The mission changes the Command beat: the player
  organizes two brigades and a crossing, not a single section.

## Objectives

### Primary

- Combine the southern and São Paulo forces in western Paraná.
- Organize the Rio Grande and São Paulo brigades under one division.
- Cross the Paraná with the division's men, horses and guns.
- Keep the division intact and prevent the government from cutting the crossing.

### Optional

- Save part of the horse herd.
- Keep both brigades together through the crossing.
- Delay the government pursuit.
- Bring the division's guns across; artillery remains out of play.

### Failure

- The two forces do not unite.
- The government cuts the crossing.
- Miguel Costa or Prestes falls or is captured.
- The division is broken or dispersed.

## Map and terrain

- Western Paraná, April 1925: Benjamin Constant, Porto Santa Helena, Porto
  Mendes and Puerto Adela, with Foz do Iguaçu as the political headquarters. The
  crossing is on the Paraná, from Porto Mendes into Paraguay.
- The map is a river crossing with a road, woods, a ferry or ford, the division's
  convoy and the government pursuit. The river is the gate; the far bank is the
  objective.
- Cover: the river bank, the woods, the ferry and the convoy. The open road is
  dangerous; the crossing is a chokepoint.
- The map teaches the difference between a column and a division: two brigades,
  one command, one crossing. The real local geography is in the sources; this
  layout is a game abstraction.

## Beats

### Briefing

Western Paraná, April 1925. The southern column and the São Paulo column meet;
Prestes and Miguel Costa agree at Benjamin Constant. Miguel Costa takes formal
command; Prestes becomes chief of staff. The
government is coming. The division will cross the Paraná before the border
closes. The player is told that the two brigades must become one division.

### Opening

The two columns arrive. The player organizes the Rio Grande and São Paulo
brigades, assigns the crossing order and screens the roads. The first government
patrols appear.

### Mid-mission

The crossing begins. The horses, the guns and the wounded compete for the boats
and the ford. The government presses the rearguard. The player must keep the two
brigades together and the crossing moving.

### Climax

The last units cross. The government reaches the bank. The player holds the near
bank long enough for the division to get across, then crosses with Miguel Costa
and the rearguard.

### Debrief

The First Revolutionary Division is across. Miguel Costa is the formal
commander; Prestes is chief of staff and the division's most visible leader. The
cost is counted: men, horses, guns, supplies. The march continues.

## Victory and defeat

- **Victory:** the two forces unite, the division is organized, and the crossing
  is completed with Miguel Costa and at least one rearguard section. The division
  is intact enough to continue the march.
- **Defeat:** the forces do not unite, the crossing is cut, Miguel Costa or
  Prestes falls, or the division is broken. Retry from deployment.
- The debrief must state the formal command: Miguel Costa commanded; Prestes was
  chief of staff.
