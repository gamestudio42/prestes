## Pitch

The São Paulo revolt has lost the city. The rebels withdraw toward the interior
and the Paraná, and the government follows with artillery. Isidoro Dias Lopes
directs the front from Foz do Iguaçu; the player commands a section under
Estilac Leal’s brigade at Catanduvas. The section holds a trench line in the
Paraná forest and farmland long enough for the wounded and the supplies to get
away. The front is lost; the men who survive will become the São Paulo remnant
that meets the southern column in western Paraná in April 1925.

Target length: 15-20 minutes at 1x, in one sitting.

## Historical frame

The São Paulo revolt began on 5 July 1924 under retired colonel Isidoro Dias
Lopes. Legalist forces retook the city by late July; the rebels withdrew toward
the interior and the Paraná River. The Paraná front held trench lines in the
forest and farmland, including the Medeiros hills and Cordeiro's trenches on the
Porto Feliz trail. Catanduvas was held by Estilac Leal's brigade; Rondon's
attacks on 26-29 March 1925 surrounded the stronghold, and 105-mm howitzers
battered it. The 407 rebels surrendered; Estilac Leal and two other officers
slipped through the lines. The remnant withdrew and met the southern column in
western Paraná in April 1925. Isidoro commanded the revolt from Foz do Iguaçu,
not from the Catanduvas trenches.

### Historical, dramatized, fictional

- **Historical:** the July 1924 São Paulo revolt; Isidoro Dias Lopes as overall
  commander; the legalist retaking of the city; the withdrawal toward the
  interior and the Paraná; the Paraná front; Estilac Leal's brigade at
  Catanduvas; the government artillery; the remnants that meet the column in
  April 1925.
- **Dramatized:** the exact trench line, the bombardment, the assault timing,
  the withdrawal and the dialogue.
- **Fictional:** the individual soldiers of the rearguard and the exact order of
  the supplies. The game map is a fictional battlefield in a sourced setting.

## Perspective

Isidoro Dias Lopes, the São Paulo revolt's commander, at Foz do Iguaçu. The
player commands a section under Estilac Leal on the Catanduvas front; Isidoro is
the command voice above it. The player sees a lost front from the section side:
the city is gone, the government has artillery, and the only victory left is to
save the men who will become the column. This is a flashback or parallel front;
it explains the São Paulo remnant before the western Paraná combination.

## Player fantasy and pillar

- **Fantasy:** Hold a lost front long enough to save the men who will become the
  column.
- **Pillar:** 1, command the section, not the hero; 2, read the ground; 3, the
  march costs more than the battle.
- **Loop beat:** Decide. The mission changes the Decide beat: the front cannot be
  held; the choice is when to leave and what to save.

## Objectives

### Primary

- Hold the trench line until the wounded and supplies are withdrawn.
- Evacuate the wounded.
- Destroy or abandon the supplies that cannot be carried.
- Withdraw the rearguard in order.
- Delay the government pursuit.

### Optional

- Keep the sections together.
- Save the guns or the stores; artillery remains out of play.
- Hold the last line long enough for the main body to get a march.

### Failure

- The government breaks the line before the withdrawal.
- The wounded or the supplies are lost.
- The rearguard is cut off.
- The section commander falls or is captured.
- The front collapses without an orderly withdrawal.

## Map and terrain

- The Paraná front, 1924-1925: a fictional battlefield in the forest and
  farmland of the Paraná plateau, with trenches, coffee rows, woods, a farmhouse
  and a withdrawal road.
- The government comes from the city side; the rebels withdraw toward the
  interior. The artillery is off-map and out of play; its pressure is scripted.
- Cover: trenches, walls, coffee rows, woods and the farmhouse. The open fields
  are dangerous; the road is the withdrawal.
- The map teaches a fighting withdrawal: hold the line, trade ground, save the
  men. The historical front is the setting, not a traced map.

## Beats

### Briefing

The city is lost. The front is lost. Isidoro’s orders reach the front from Foz;
Estilac Leal’s brigade holds Catanduvas. The rearguard is told to hold the trench
line until the wounded and supplies are through, then withdraw in order. The
player is told that the front is not the objective; the men are.

### Opening

The government bombards the line. The rearguard takes the trenches. The first
assault probes the wire and the coffee rows.

### Mid-mission

The government presses the line. The wounded move back. The player rotates the
sections, holds the farmhouse and the road, and decides what to abandon.

### Climax

The last line. The main body is away. The player withdraws under pressure,
trading the trench for the road, and gets the rearguard out.

### Debrief

The front falls. The survivors move toward the interior and the Paraná. In April
1925 the São Paulo remnant will meet the southern column in western Paraná. The
front is lost; the men are saved.

## Victory and defeat

- **Victory:** the wounded and supplies are withdrawn, the rearguard gets out in
  order, and the front is given up without a rout. The men survive to join the
  column.
- **Defeat:** the line breaks before the withdrawal, the wounded or supplies are
  lost, the rearguard is cut off, the section commander falls or is captured, or
  the front collapses without order. Retry from deployment.
- The debrief must not call the outcome a victory. The front is lost; the men are
  saved.
