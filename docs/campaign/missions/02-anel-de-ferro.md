# 02 - O Anel de Ferro

## Pitch

The government believes it has the southern rising in a ring. Prestes knows a
ring is only a line. The player commands the rearguard while the column leaves
São Luiz Gonzaga, holds Ramada long enough for the wagons to pass, and crosses
the Uruguay River before the ring closes. The column survives, but it loses most
of its horses and continues on foot. This is where the rising becomes a column.

Target length: 20-25 minutes at 1x, in one sitting.

## Historical frame

The state-wide revolt in Rio Grande do Sul began on 28-29 October 1924. Rebels
took Santo Ângelo, São Borja and Uruguaiana; Juarez Távora was among the leaders
at Uruguaiana. Prestes was serving with the 1st Railway Battalion at Santo
Ângelo. Government forces converged on São Luiz Gonzaga in December. Prestes
avoided the encirclement, marched north, and fought the Combat of Ramada on 3
January 1925 near Palmeiras. He reached the Uruguay River on 9 January and
crossed later in the month; the column lost most of its horses and continued on
foot through Santa Catarina. In April 1925 the southern rebels met the São Paulo
remnants in western Paraná; Prestes and Miguel Costa met at Benjamin Constant on
3 April, and the First Revolutionary Division was formally organized at Porto
Santa Helena on 14 April, with Miguel Costa as formal commander and Prestes as
chief of staff.

### Historical, dramatized, fictional

- **Historical:** the October 1924 rising; Santo Ângelo, São Borja and
  Uruguaiana; the government convergence on São Luiz Gonzaga; Prestes's
  avoidance of the encirclement; Ramada on 3 January 1925; the Uruguay crossing;
  the loss of most horses; the march on foot through Santa Catarina; the April
  1925 combination in western Paraná and the First Revolutionary Division.
- **Dramatized:** the exact route, the night march, the rearguard positions,
  the timing of the government columns, the river crossing and the dialogue.
- **Fictional:** the individual soldiers of the rearguard and the exact order of
  the wagons and horses. The column is represented by a playable slice, not by
  its full historical strength.
- **Legend versus record:** the ring is remembered as the moment the column was
  born. The record also shows the cost: horses, supplies, men and the decision to
  abandon what could not cross.

## Perspective

Luís Carlos Prestes, chief of staff of the southern rebels. The player sees the
ring from inside: the government columns converging, the column's wagons and
horses slowing the march, and the river as the only gate. Prestes is not a
superhero; he is the officer who chooses to move instead of waiting to be
surrounded. The mission is the first step of the column that will later be led
formally by Miguel Costa.

## Player fantasy and pillar

- **Fantasy:** Turn a trapped rising into a column that cannot be surrounded.
- **Pillar:** 1, command the section, not the hero; 2, read the ground before the
  fight; 3, the march costs more than the battle.
- **Loop beat:** Decide. The mission changes the Decide beat: holding a town is
  not victory; breaking the ring and keeping the column moving is.

## Objectives

### Primary

- Screen the column's departure from São Luiz Gonzaga.
- Hold the Ramada rearguard until the wagons reach the river.
- Cross the Uruguay with Prestes and at least one section.
- Prevent the government from cutting the route north.

### Optional

- Bring the wounded across.
- Save part of the horse herd; the record says most were lost.
- Keep the rearguard together.
- Delay the pursuit long enough to gain a march.

### Failure

- The government closes the ring before the column leaves.
- The Ramada line collapses before the wagons pass.
- The Uruguay crossing is lost.
- Prestes falls or is captured.
- The column is destroyed.

## Map and terrain

- Southern Rio Grande do Sul, December 1924 to January 1925: São Luiz Gonzaga,
  the road north, the Ramada position and the Uruguay River crossing.
- The map is a northward route with fields, woods, a road, a stream and a ford.
  The government comes from the south and east; the column moves north and west.
- Cover: tree lines, fences, the stream bank, the ford and the wagons. The open
  fields are dangerous; the woods and the river bank are the only real cover.
- The map teaches the difference between holding a town and keeping a column
  moving. The real local geography is in the sources; this layout is a game
  abstraction.

## Beats

### Briefing

São Luiz Gonzaga, December 1924. The government is closing the ring. Prestes
refuses to wait for the encirclement: the column will march north at night, the
rearguard will hold Ramada, and the Uruguay is the gate. The player is told that
the town is not the objective; the column is.

### Opening

The column leaves. The player screens the roads and the flanks. A government
patrol probes the route. The player chooses where to hold and where to fall back.

### Mid-mission

The government brings up more troops. The wagons and horses slow the column.
The player holds Ramada, rotates the sections, and decides what to abandon. The
wounded and the horses compete for time the column does not have.

### Climax

The column reaches the Uruguay. The crossing is a ferry and a shallow ford. The government presses the
rearguard. The player must hold the bank long enough for the wagons to cross,
then cross with Prestes and the surviving section. The horses are lost or left
behind.

### Debrief

The ring is broken. The column survives, but the cost is counted: men, horses,
supplies and the towns left behind. The column continues on foot through Santa
Catarina. In April it will meet the São Paulo rebels in western Paraná; Miguel
Costa will take formal command. The march has begun.

## Victory and defeat

- **Victory:** the column leaves São Luiz Gonzaga, the Ramada rearguard holds
  long enough for the wagons, and Prestes crosses the Uruguay with at least one
  section. The ring is broken, but the cost is counted.
- **Defeat:** the ring closes, the Ramada line collapses, the crossing is lost,
  Prestes falls or is captured, or the column is destroyed. Retry from
  deployment.
- The debrief must not call the outcome a clean victory. The column survives;
  the horses do not. The next mission begins in western Paraná.

## Out of scope

- The western Paraná combination and the First Revolutionary Division (mission 03).
- The full march, the Northeast, the Pantanal and the exile.
- Playable cavalry, artillery, machine guns or a new weapon system.
- Miguel Costa as a playable figure in this mission; he joins in western Paraná.
- Campaign progression, save/load and between-mission UI.
- A full historical reconstruction of Ramada and the crossing; the mission is a
  dramatized slice.
