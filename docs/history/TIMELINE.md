## Divergences (enrich-texts-from-sources)

- **When the column began.** The baseline dates the column to the division
  formed in western Paraná on 14 April 1925 (Macaulay, ch. III). Anita
  Prestes (thesis, 1989; `anita-1991`) and Amilcar Guidolim Vitor
  (`historiafm-2024`, `vitor-2011`) date it to the departure from São Luiz
  Gonzaga on 27 December 1924. The game keeps Macaulay's chronology and the
  compendium names both views.
- **_O Libertador_, how many issues.** Macaulay (ch. VI, p. 165; ch. VII,
  pp. 172, 196): six at São Luís in late 1924, the seventh at Porto Nacional
  (Oct. 1925), the eighth at Carolina and the ninth at Floriano. Anita
  Prestes (_Brasil de Fato_, 2024): five at São Luís Gonzaga and four later,
  nine in all. The _Brasil Cultura_ caption (2009): ten in all.
  `historiafm-2024`: five at São Luiz Gonzaga. The first issue is dated
  November or December 1924 depending on the source. The compendium gives the
  counts as a range, not a single number.
- **How many states.** Thirteen (CPDOC, Anita Prestes) or fifteen (the
  _Brasil Cultura_ page counts RS, SC and PR plus twelve). The game says
  thirteen, with 1920s borders, and notes the other count.
- **Where the women stayed behind.** Veiga (1992, via `mozzer-2009`) and
  `historiafm-2024` have the women crossing a river into Santa Catarina
  against Prestes's order. Veiga does not name the river; the podcast names
  the Uruguay. Macaulay places the crossing of the Uruguay in January 1925
  (ch. III, pp. 74–76).
- **What the women did.** Macaulay (ch. VI, pp. 147–148) counts more than
  fifty women, almost all from Rio Grande do Sul, and says few carried
  firearms or fought; his portrait of them is dismissive. Carvalho
  (`carvalho-2001`, as summarized in `historiafm-2024`) and Vitor argue they
  fought, nursed and scouted, and that the period accounts, Moreira Lima's
  above all, reduced them. The compendium gives both readings. It takes
  Macaulay's count and his named cases (Santa Rosa, Hermínia, Tia Maria) and
  does not repeat his judgements of the women's looks or morals.
- **Tia Maria's death.** Macaulay (p. 147) has her as an elderly Black cook
  with the rebels since São Paulo and says nothing of her death. Amado (via
  `mozzer-2009`) says she died under torture; `historiafm-2024` says
  government troops made her dig her own grave before shooting her. Recorded
  as tradition, not as a checked fact.

## Known corrections

- **Prestes was not at the Forte de Copacabana.** He was ill with typhoid on
  4–5 July 1922. The Prologue is played as Siqueira Campos and the rebels, not
  as Prestes.
- **The “18” is a legend.** The fort held 29; 28 marched out; 16 reached the
  government line; Siqueira and two others survived. The game may use the legend
  of the eighteen, but not as a head count.
- **São Paulo 1924 was a separate front.** The column formed in western Paraná
  from the remnants of the São Paulo revolt and the southern rebels; it did not
  fight in São Paulo city as a column.
- **The junction was not at Foz do Iguaçu.** Prestes and Miguel Costa met at
  Benjamin Constant on 3 April 1925; the division formed at Porto Santa Helena
  on 14 April; Foz was Isidoro’s political headquarters.
- **Isidoro was not in the Catanduvas trenches.** Estilac Leal’s brigade held
  Catanduvas; Isidoro commanded the revolt from Foz.
- **Miguel Costa was the formal commander.** Prestes was chief of staff and the
  column’s most visible leader; the game must not erase the command structure.
- **Serra Nova was a rearguard ambush, not a pass crossing.** The column escaped
  by breaking contact and maneuvering, not by holding a highland pass.
- **Use 1920s political borders.** Tocantins did not exist; the column crossed
  northern Goiás, today’s Tocantins. Maps and codex use historical borders.
- **Macaulay is a 1974 American academic interpretation.** Brazilian
  historiography and primary memoirs may differ; the codex records alternatives
  rather than pretending there is one neutral account.
